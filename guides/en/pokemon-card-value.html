1. Identify your exact card

Two cards of the same Pokémon can be worth 10 cents or several hundred euros. First, you need to know exactly which card you have:

  • The number at the bottom of the card (for example 25/165): its place in the set.
  • The set symbol next to the number, or the set code on recent cards.
  • The language of the card.

2. What makes a card valuable

  • Rarity: the symbol at the bottom (circle, diamond, star…) and the finish (holo, full art, gold).
  • Condition: often what matters most. A perfect card can be worth several times the same card with light wear.
  • Language: the same card does not have the same price in Japanese, English or French.
  • Edition: on the very first sets, the "1st Edition" stamp or a "shadowless" print changes the price a lot.
  • Demand: the most loved Pokémon (Charizard, Pikachu, Eevee and its evolutions…) and beautiful artwork sell for more.

3. Check the condition

Put the card on a dark background, under good light, and look at:

  • the corners: sharp, or white and rounded?
  • the edges, especially on the back: any small white marks?
  • the surface: scratches, marks, creases, tilting the card towards the light;
  • the centring: are the borders the same width on both sides?

Marketplaces use a common scale: Mint, Near Mint, Excellent, Good, Light Played, Played, Poor. A card pulled from a booster and sleeved straight away is usually Near Mint.

4. Look at real sales, not listings

A listing at €500 means nothing: anyone can ask any price. What counts is the price the card actually sells for, in the same language and condition.

  • On Cardmarket, check the price trend and the latest sales.
  • On eBay, filter for sold items: search for your card on eBay.
  • Compare several sales, not just one.

5. What if your card is worth a lot?

Put it in a sleeve and a toploader right away. If it is in excellent condition, grading can raise its value and reassure a buyer: see grading your cards. Before buying an expensive card, watch out for fakes: see how to spot a fake card.

Frequently asked questions

Are common cards worth anything?

One by one, very little: a few cents. They sell in bulk lots. The exceptions are very old commons in perfect condition, and promos.

Is a Japanese card worth more than an English one?

Not necessarily: it depends on the card. Some are pricier in Japanese, others in English. Always compare the same language.

Is my old 1999 card worth a fortune?

Only if it is rare, in demand and in excellent condition. Most cards from that era are worth a few euros, but some holos in perfect condition are worth much more.
