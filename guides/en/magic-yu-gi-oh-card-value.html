Magic: The Gathering

Reading the rarity

The colour of the set symbol, on the right under the artwork, shows the rarity: black (common), silver (uncommon), gold (rare), orange (mythic rare).

The cards worth a lot

  • The very first editions (Alpha, Beta, Unlimited) and old, very powerful cards, some of which will never be reprinted.
  • Cards played in Commander, the most popular format: player demand pushes prices up.
  • Special versions: foils, alternate arts, serialised cards.

The same card can exist in dozens of versions with very different prices: check the exact set.

Yu-Gi-Oh!

Reading the code

Under the artwork, on the right, a code like LOB-EN001 shows the set, language and number. The words «1st Edition» change the price.

The rarities

From most common to rarest: Common, Rare, Super Rare, Ultra Rare, Secret Rare, then special rarities such as Ultimate Rare, Ghost Rare, Starlight Rare or Quarter Century Secret Rare. Silver or gold lettering on the name and a shiny artwork help you recognise them.

The cards worth a lot

  • Old 1st Edition cards, especially iconic monsters (Blue-Eyes White Dragon, Dark Magician…).
  • Special rarities of cards played in tournaments.
  • Rare promos and tournament cards.

For both games

  • Condition matters as much as for Pokémon: corners, edges, surface.
  • Look at actual sales, not listings: see the cards on eBay.
  • Yu-Gi-Oh! cards are smaller: you need «small» sleeves.

Frequently asked questions

Are my old Yu-Gi-Oh! cards from the 2000s worth anything?

Most are worth little, but 1st Editions of iconic cards in good condition can be worth a lot. Check the code and the edition text.

Does a reprinted Magic card lose value?

A reprint often lowers the price of the old common versions, but first editions and rare versions usually hold their value.
