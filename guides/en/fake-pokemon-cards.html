2. Text and font

Fakes often have slightly different text: a bolder or thinner font, badly spaced letters, missing accents, spelling mistakes, poorly drawn energy symbols. Some cards printed for export mix several languages.

3. Colours

Colours that are too pale or too saturated, a blurry image, smudged outlines: a counterfeit's print quality is usually lower. Compare with a card you know is real.

4. The back of the card

A real card's back has a deep blue with crisp gradients. On many fakes the blue leans purple or light, and the logo is less sharp.

5. Holo and texture

Holo cards have a precise effect that only covers certain areas. The rarest recent cards (full arts, gold cards) also have a texture you can feel with your finger. A smooth, evenly shiny "gold" card is almost always fake.

6. Thickness and stiffness

A real card has a characteristic stiffness. Fakes are often too thin and floppy, or too thick, with an overly glossy or laminated surface.

7. The light test

Shine your phone's torch through the card from behind. A real card lets little light through, thanks to a dark layer inside the card stock. Many fakes let light through evenly. Do the test next to a real common card from the same era.

8. Graded cards

Fake grading slabs exist too. Always check the certification number on the grading company's website (PSA, PCA, CCC, BGS, CGC…): the card, grade and photo must match. Check the quality of the slab and label as well.

Before buying online

  • A price far below the market value should ring alarm bells.
  • Ask for clear photos of the front and back, in natural light.
  • Check the seller's feedback and prefer a protected payment.
  • Beware of cheap "100 cards including 10 gold" lots.

Frequently asked questions

Are fake cards illegal?

Selling them as genuine is fraud. "Custom" cards sold clearly as such are not official cards and have no collector value.

What if I bought a fake?

Contact the seller, then open a dispute on the marketplace if needed. Keep the photos and messages.
