Football and F1 cards: which are worth money?

Updated 27 November 2026

Sports cards have their own codes: rookie cards, numbered cards, autographs. Here is how to find the valuable ones in your Panini or Topps packs.

1. What makes a card valuable

  • The rookie card (RC): a player's or driver's first official card. Often the most sought-after of their career.
  • Numbered cards: a stamp such as 25/99 means a limited print run. The lower the number, the rarer the card; a 1/1 is unique.
  • Autographs, signed on the card or on a sticker.
  • Patches and relics, with a piece of shirt or race suit.
  • Parallels: same card, different border or colour (refractor, gold, red…), rarer.
  • The player: stars and young talents set the prices.

2. Panini, Topps: finding your way

  • Football: Panini (stickers, Adrenalyn XL cards and premium collections) and Topps (including the Chrome lines).
  • F1: the licence belongs to Topps, with lines such as Chrome and Turbo Attax cards for younger fans.

Kids' game cards (Adrenalyn XL, Turbo Attax) are usually worth little, apart from limited ones. «Premium» lines contain more numbered and autographed cards.

3. Valuing a card

There is no free public price guide like for Pokémon: the reference is actual sales. Search for the player's name, line, year, parallel and number, then look at sold items: see the cards on eBay affiliate link.

Condition matters hugely: chrome cards show scratches and worn corners straight away. Valuable cards are often graded (PSA, BGS, SGC…).

Frequently asked questions

Is a numbered card always worth a lot?

No: a numbered card of a little-known player sometimes sells for a few euros. The player and the rarity of the print run count together.

Should I leave cards in their wrapper?

Take them out of the pack and put them straight into a protective sleeve, then a toploader for the best ones.
