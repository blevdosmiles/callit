Estimating your card's PSA grade

Updated 27 November 2026

Before paying for grading, you can get a good idea of the grade your card can aim for. Here is what PSA looks at, the centring limits to know and a simple way to estimate your card.

1. How PSA grades a card

PSA gives a single grade from 1 to 10, sometimes with a half point (8.5 for example). There are no subgrades: the final grade depends on four criteria, and it is often the weakest one that sets it.

  • Centring: the difference between the borders, front and back.
  • Corners: sharp, with no whitening or bends.
  • Edges: no white specks or chips, especially on the back.
  • Surface: no scratches, marks, dents or print defects.

A perfectly centred card with one whitened corner will not get a 10.

2. Centring: the one criterion you can measure

Centring is written as two numbers, for example 55/45: the wider border makes up 55% of the two added together. You measure left-right and top-bottom, and keep the worse of the two.

Example: left border 2.2 mm, right border 1.8 mm. Total 4 mm, the wider one is 2.2 ÷ 4 = 55%, so 55/45.

The worst centring PSA accepts for each grade:

PSA 1055/45 on the front, 75/25 on the back
PSA 960/40 on the front, 90/10 on the back
PSA 865/35 on the front, 90/10 on the back
PSA 770/30 on the front, 90/10 on the back
PSA 680/20 on the front, 90/10 on the back
PSA 585/15 on the front, 90/10 on the back

The back is far more forgiving: the front almost always decides. Centring outside the limits caps the grade, even if everything else is perfect.

3. Corners, edges and surface: look closely

  1. Take the card out of its sleeve and lay it on a clean, dark background.
  2. Light it from the side with a low lamp: raking light shows up scratches, dents and fingerprints.
  3. Check corners and edges with a magnifier, front then back. The smallest white speck counts.
  4. Tilt the card under the light to check the surface, especially on shiny and holo cards.

For the details, a magnifier with a light affiliate link helps a lot.

A few rules of thumb:

  • Aiming for a 10: nothing visible under magnification, centring within the limits.
  • Aiming for a 9: one very small flaw, such as a barely visible white speck on an edge.
  • Aiming for an 8 or lower: several small flaws, a slightly worn corner or a scratch visible to the naked eye.
  • A crease, even a light one, usually drops the card much lower.

5. What next?

A good estimated grade does not mean you should send the card. Grading costs money and takes time: it is mostly worth it for cards that are already valuable or rare. CallIt!'s "Worth grading?" tool compares your card's price with the cost of grading.

Frequently asked questions

How reliable is an estimate?

It gives a good idea, not a guarantee. A photo measures to within 1 or 2 points, and some surface flaws only show with the card in hand. Only the grading company gives the official grade.

Are the limits the same at other companies?

No, each company has its own scale. For a 10, CGC asks like PSA for 55/45 on the front and 75/25 on the back, Beckett (BGS) for 50/50 on the front and 60/40 on the back. Always check the scale on the official site of the company you choose.

My cards are fresh from the pack: are they automatically 10s?

No. Many new cards have off-centre printing, a small print defect or an edge damaged when cut. A new card needs checking like any other.
