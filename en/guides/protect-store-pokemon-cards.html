Protecting and storing your Pokémon cards

Updated 27 November 2026

A damaged card quickly loses value, and a whitened corner cannot be fixed. Here is how to protect each card according to its value, and organise your collection so you can find everything.

1. The right gear for each card

  • Common cards: a proper storage box, cards standing up, no rubber bands (they dent cards).
  • Cards you love: a soft sleeve, then a binder.
  • Valuable cards: soft sleeve, then a toploader or a magnetic holder.
  • Graded cards: standing up in a box made for slabs, safe from knocks.

2. Choosing a binder

  • Prefer a binder with stitched pages to a ring binder: rings can dent cards near the spine.
  • Side-loading pockets stop cards from sliding out.
  • Don't overfill it: a card forced into a pocket will bend.
  • A zip closure keeps dust out better.

For example: side-loading binders with a zip affiliate link.

3. The right conditions

  • A dry place: humidity makes cards curl.
  • Away from sunlight, which fades colours.
  • No basement, loft or windowsill.
  • Handle cards with clean, dry hands.

4. Organising your collection

Pick an order and stick to it: by set then number (the easiest way to complete a set), by Pokémon, by type or by language. Leave empty slots for missing cards: you can see at a glance what you still need.

5. Shipping a card safely

  1. Soft sleeve, then toploader.
  2. A small piece of tape to close the toploader (easy to remove), or a resealable bag.
  3. All of it between two rigid pieces of cardboard, in a padded envelope.
  4. Tracked shipping, insured for valuable cards.

Frequently asked questions

What size sleeves for Pokémon cards?

Pokémon, Magic and One Piece cards use the standard size: "standard" sleeves of about 66 × 91 mm. Yu-Gi-Oh! cards are smaller and need "small" or "Japanese size" sleeves.

Can I flatten a curved card?

A slight curve often goes away if the card rests flat in a toploader for a few days, somewhere dry. Avoid heat and heavy weights.
