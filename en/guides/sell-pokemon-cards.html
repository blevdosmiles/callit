Where and how to sell your Pokémon cards

Updated 27 November 2026

Selling a card for the right price is mostly about choosing where to sell it, describing it well and shipping it well. Here are the options, with their pros and pitfalls.

1. Where to sell?

  • Cardmarket: the big European marketplace between collectors. Lots of buyers, reference prices, fees on each sale.
  • eBay: ideal for valuable and graded cards, at auction or fixed price, with buyers worldwide.
  • Vinted and peer-to-peer sites: simple for bundles and common cards; the buyer pays for protection.
  • Shops: instant buyback, no shipping, but at a lower price than the selling price.
  • Groups and fairs: good visibility, but read the group rules and prefer hand delivery or protected payment.

2. Setting the right price

Start from actual sales of the same card, in the same language and condition, not from listings. For common cards, match the market; for a rare card, an auction can push the price up. Don't forget fees and postage.

3. Describe and photograph

  • Sharp photos of the front and back, on a plain background, without glare.
  • Close-ups of the corners and any flaws: a reassured buyer buys faster.
  • An honest condition (Near Mint, Excellent…), language, set and number.
  • No text or logos on the photos.

4. Ship safely

  1. Penny sleeve, then a toploader.
  2. Between two pieces of stiff card, in a padded envelope.
  3. Tracked shipping above a few euros or pounds, insured for valuable cards.

The guide how to protect and store Pokémon cards explains packing.

5. Common cards

Sold one by one, they sell poorly. Group them into bundles (by set, by type or by weight): that is what parents, beginners and players look for.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to declare my sales?

In the European Union, platforms report sellers above 30 sales or €2,000 a year to the tax authorities; the UK has a similar rule (30 sales or about £1,700). Selling your own personal collection is not a business, but check your country's rules if you sell a lot.

Auction or fixed price?

Fixed price for common cards with a known value; auction for rare, sought-after cards, ideally ending on a weekend evening.
