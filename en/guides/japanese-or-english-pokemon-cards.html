Japanese or English Pokémon cards?

Updated 27 November 2026

Same Pokémon, same artwork, but not the same price or the same feel in hand. Here is what really changes from one language to another, so you can choose with confidence.

1. Releases don't follow the same calendar

Cards come out in Japan first. International versions (English, French, German, Italian, Spanish…) arrive weeks or months later, often in sets organised differently: one international set can combine cards from several Japanese sets.

As a result, the same card can have a different number and set name depending on the language.

On CallIt!, a card page links all its versions, whatever the language or set.

2. What changes in hand

  • The back: Japanese cards have a different back from international cards.
  • The print: Japanese cards have a reputation for better printing and centring, which matters for grading.
  • The packs: Japanese packs usually hold fewer cards and cost less per pack.
  • Chinese cards (simplified) are a separate family, often cheaper, with products found only in China.

3. Price

There is no fixed rule. The same card can be more expensive in Japanese, English or another language depending on its rarity in each language, local demand and how many were printed. English cards have the biggest market; other European languages are mostly sought in their own country, and some older ones have become scarce.

Always compare the same card, in the same language and the same condition.

4. Resale

  • English: the easiest to resell anywhere, and the most followed.
  • Japanese: highly sought by collectors and for grading.
  • French, German, Italian, Spanish: resell mostly in their own country, ideal if you collect for yourself.

5. So, which one?

The one you love. If you grew up with English cards, nostalgia counts. If you like beautiful prints and cheap packs to open, Japanese is a good choice. If you might resell one day, English and Japanese are the most liquid. What matters is staying consistent: one or two languages, not five.

Frequently asked questions

Can you play Japanese cards in tournaments?

Official tournaments in Europe and the UK usually accept cards in the international languages. Check the event rules before you go.

Why are Japanese packs cheaper?

They hold fewer cards and have a lower retail price in Japan. With importing and demand, the gap varies a lot from one set to another.
